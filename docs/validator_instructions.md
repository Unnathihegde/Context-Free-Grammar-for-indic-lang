# Telugu Agreement Validation Instructions

Thank you for helping validate sentences and minimal pairs for the Telugu grammar project.

The validation process consists of two stages:
1. **Sentence-level Naturalness Screening** (`docs/validation_by_sentence.csv`)
2. **Pair-level Contrast Validation** (`docs/validation_sheet.csv`)

---

## Stage 1: Sentence-Level Naturalness Check (`docs/validation_by_sentence.csv`)

Before checking perturbations, we must ensure that the base sentences from the treebank are natural Telugu sentences.

The file `docs/validation_by_sentence.csv` contains the **66 distinct good sentences** from which candidate pairs were generated.

### Columns to Fill:
- **`good_sentence_id`**: Identifier (`sent_001` to `sent_066`, do not edit).
- **`good_sentence`**: Original sentence from the Telugu treebank.
- **`good_sentence_natural`**: **Mark `yes` or `no`**.
  - **`yes`**: The sentence is natural, grammatical Telugu.
  - **`no`**: The sentence feels unnatural, artificial, or ungrammatical in standard Telugu.
  - **Important**: If marked `no`, **all bad variant pairs generated from this sentence are automatically excluded**.
- **`validator_notes`**: Any comments on register, awkward phrasing, or dialectal nuance.

---

## Stage 2: Pair-Level Contrast Check (`docs/validation_sheet.csv`)

The file `docs/validation_sheet.csv` contains **251 candidate minimal pairs** generated by swapping verb endings to induce subject–verb person/number agreement clashes.

### Columns in `docs/validation_sheet.csv`:
- **`pair_id`**: Identifier (e.g., `agreement_0001`).
- **`good_sentence`**: The grammatical sentence.
- **`bad_sentence`**: The perturbed candidate sentence.
- **`subject`**: Subject pronoun (e.g., `నేను`, `నువ్వు`, `అతను`).
- **`good_ending`**: The original verb suffix.
- **`bad_ending`**: The substituted verb suffix.
- **`ending_variants`**: Alternate orthographic forms of the same ending (e.g. `-ాం, -ాము` for 1st person plural).
- **`risk_note`**: Set to `honorific_possible` for `-ారు`, alerting you that this ending can indicate polite/honorific agreement for 2nd/3rd person singular subjects.
- **`validated`**: **Mark `yes` or `no`**.
- **`validator_notes`**: Optional notes explaining borderline cases.

### Validation Decision Rules:
1. **Mark `yes` only if:**
   - The first sentence (`good_sentence`) is natural and grammatical.
   - AND the second sentence (`bad_sentence`) is **clearly ungrammatical / wrong** due to a subject–verb agreement clash.
2. **Mark `no` if:**
   - The second sentence (`bad_sentence`) could be **acceptable in any register** (including colloquial, formal, polite, honorific, or regional variants).
   - In particular, pay attention to rows with `risk_note: honorific_possible` (`-ారు`): if the sentence could be interpreted as a respectful/honorific construction for the subject, mark `no`.
   - OR if the first sentence itself is questionable.
3. **Add a note in `validator_notes` if unsure:**
   - Flag any subtle dialectal or context-dependent acceptability.

---

## Authoring New Speaker-Written Sentences & Pairs

To achieve reportable conclusions on agreement, the project requires **at least 30 distinct validated good sentences** authored by a native Telugu speaker (stored in `data/agreement/agreement_pairs_validated.csv` with `source=speaker_written`).

When writing new sentences:
- Create natural, everyday Telugu sentences with explicit subject pronouns (`నేను`, `మేము`/`మనం`, `నువ్వు`, `మీరు`, `అతను`, `ఆమె`, `వారు`).
- For each good sentence, create 1 to 3 bad sentences by altering only the verb suffix to cause unambiguous person/number clashes.
- Ensure coverage across past, present, and future tenses.
